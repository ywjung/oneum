# OnEum User Guide

## The Name OnEum
**OnEum** is the Korean word **온음** written in Latin letters. Say it "on-eum".
- **On** (온) is an old Korean word for "whole, all of it", as in "the whole world" or "all day long", and **eum** (音) means sound. Together: hearing the music you have whole, complete and as it is. The principles of this program come from there: your files are never changed, songs are not cut apart, and the sound is only processed by what you switch on yourself.
- In music, **온음** is a whole tone (two semitones), and a whole note is the longest-held note. The app icon is a whole note on a staff.
- It also sounds like the English **on**: add a folder, and the music is on.

## Getting Started
1. Open OnEum and click **Add Music Folder** (or drag a music folder onto the window or the Dock icon).
2. The music in the folder starts appearing in the list. There is no need to wait for the scan to finish: **double-click** a song to play it.
3. You can add several folders. When files are added to them or removed, the list updates by itself.

- You can also open music files without adding a folder: **File → Open Music Files…** (⌘O), or drag the files onto the window or the Dock icon. They become the queue and start playing, and stay in the library as individually added songs.
- OnEum only reads your music files. It never changes or deletes files or tags. Favorites, play counts, playlists and the loudness the app measured are all kept in the app's own data.
- No account and no internet connection are needed. Lyrics, cover art and song details are shown only from what is in the files and their folders.
- You can open this guide again at any time from **Help → OnEum User Guide** in the menu bar. Click an entry on the left to go to that section; press Esc to close.

## The Window
- **Left**: Songs · Albums · Artists · Genres · Favorites · Listen Again · Folders · Statistics, and below them your playlists (smart playlists first). Anything you pinned comes first, at the top.
- **Middle**: the song list or the album covers. The number of songs and their total length are shown at the bottom.
- **Top**: the search field on the left, and five buttons on the right — equalizer, VU meter, settings, Up Next and song info.
- **Right**: the Up Next (queue) or Song Info panel (open and close them with the buttons at the top, or with ⌘U and ⌘I).
- **Bottom**: the song now playing, shuffle · previous · play · next · repeat, the playback position and the volume. Click the cover to go to its album, or the title to find the song in the list (⌘L).
- Click an album, artist or genre to see its songs, and the **Back** button to return. **Genres** gathers the genres in your tags, with the number of songs in each (a song with several genres is in each of them).
- **Pin to Sidebar**: right-click an album, artist, genre or playlist → **Pin to Sidebar** to keep it under "Pinned" at the top on the left (up to 12). Right-click a pinned entry → **Unpin**.
- **Folders**: browse your music folders the way they are laid out on disk. Click a name in the path at the top to go to that folder, or a button below it to go into a folder inside. The list shows every song in the folder, in path order.
- **Statistics**: from the play history kept on this computer, how often and how long you listened in the last 7 days, 30 days, year or all time, with your top artists, albums, songs and genres; and what your library is made of (formats, sample rates). Click a name to go there.

## Listening
- Double-click a song (or select it and press Return): the whole list you are looking at becomes the queue, starting from that song. If you have searched or sorted, the results become the queue just as they are.
- In the album view, the ▶ button that appears when you point at a cover plays the album at once.
- Space plays and pauses. Drag or click the position bar to move within the song, and use ⌥⌘→ / ⌥⌘← to skip 5 seconds ahead or back.
- **Previous** restarts the current song once more than 3 seconds of it have played; before that it goes to the song before.
- **Repeat** cycles through Off → All → One. While one song repeats, the next and previous buttons still move to other songs.
- **Shuffle** mixes the queue with the current song kept first; switching it off restores the original order. While shuffling, "previous" is the song you just heard.
- **Shuffle by Album** (Settings → Playback → Playing Order): shuffle mixes the order of the albums, and each album plays through in order, without gaps.
- **Resume Long Tracks** (Settings → Playback → Playing Order, on at first): a track over 20 minutes (a DJ mix, an audiobook, a long classical piece) starts where you left off when you choose it again. When an album plays on into it, and once it was heard to the end, it starts from the beginning.
- **Stop After Current Song** (Controls menu, ⌘⇧., or right-click the play button): plays the current song to its end and stops. While it is set, "Stops after song" is shown next to the volume; click it to switch it off. Playback stops with the next song ready, so pressing play goes on from there. It applies to this one song only (moving to another song cancels it).
- **Repeating a section (A–B)** (**Mark Section to Repeat (A–B)** in the Controls menu, ⌘B, or right-click the play button): plays one section of a song over and over. The first time marks where the section begins (A), the second where it ends (B); from then on playback returns to the beginning whenever it reaches the end. The third time switches it off. While it is on, "Repeating 1:02–1:35" is shown next to the volume (clicking those words switches it off too), and both places are marked on the seek bar. It belongs to that one song: another song ends it, and it is not kept for the next time the app is opened.
- **Sleep Timer** (Controls → Sleep Timer, or right-click the play button): stops playback in 15 minutes, 30 minutes, 45 minutes, 1 hour or 1 hour 30 minutes. The sound fades out over the last few seconds, and your volume setting stays as it was. While it runs, the time left is shown next to the volume; click it to switch the timer off.
- Set the volume with the slider at the bottom right or with ⌘↑ / ⌘↓. The speaker icon (⌘⇧M) mutes; clicking it again brings back the volume you had.
- Click the time to the right of the position bar to switch between the total length and the time left.
- The songs of an album follow each other without gaps (see "Supported Files" for the differences between formats).
- Closing the window (⌘W) does not stop the music. Click OnEum in the Dock to open the window again. To quit, press ⌘Q.
- When you start OnEum again, the queue and the position you were at come back. Nothing plays until you press play.
- The play, previous and next keys on your keyboard and Now Playing in Control Center work as well.

## Song Lists
- Click a column title to sort by it. Click again to reverse, and once more for the original order (artist → album → track number).
- Select several songs with ⌘-click or ⇧-click; ⌘A selects all. Move with ↑↓ and extend or shrink the selection with ⇧↑↓. Home/End and Page Up/Down work too.
- Right-click a song for **Play**, **Play Next**, **Add to Queue**, **Add to Playlist**, **Add to Favorites**, **Go to Album**, **Go to Artist**, **Show in Finder** and **Song Info**. With several songs selected, playing, adding and favorites apply to all of them. **Remove from Library** takes songs out of the library and the playlists only; the files stay where they are. A scan of their folder (as at every start) does not bring them back; **Settings → Library → Bring Back Removed Songs** takes all of them in again (a file that is not cut into songs by a CUE sheet is also taken in again when you open it yourself or drop it on the window).
- The same commands are in the **Song** menu of the menu bar. They apply to the selected songs, or to the song playing when nothing is selected.
- **Favorites**: click the heart in a song's row or press ⌘D. They gather under Favorites on the left.
- Click the list once, then type: the list jumps to the first song beginning with what you typed.
- Drag songs or albums onto a playlist or onto the queue.
- **Choosing columns**: right-click a column title to switch the **Year · Genre · Plays · Added · Last Played · Format** columns on and off. The menu stays open, so you can choose several, then click outside it to close it. The Year, Plays, Added, Last Played and Format columns can be sorted by clicking their titles. In a narrow window, the last columns are hidden for the time being.
- The play count goes up by one when half of the song (4 minutes for long songs) has actually been heard. Songs that were skipped are not counted.

## Search
- Type in the search field at the top (⌘F) to find songs by title, artist and album as you type. Esc clears it. The search looks within the view that is open (Songs, an album, a playlist …).
- Part of a word is enough: `bea` finds The Beatles. Upper and lower case do not matter.
- Typing only Korean initial consonants searches by initials: `ㅇㅇㅇ` finds 아이유.
- With several words, only songs that contain all of them are shown. Put a phrase in double quotes to find it exactly: `"let it be"`.
- You can say where to look: `artist:beatles`, `album:abbey`, `title:night`, `genre:jazz`, `format:flac`, and mix this with other words: `genre:rock year:1969 sun`.
- Years can be ranges: `year:1994`, `year:1990-1999`, `year:>=2000`, `year:<1980`.
- There are also `format:lossless` (FLAC, ALAC, WAV, AIFF) and `format:lossy`. Genres are only searched with `genre:`.
- You can search by what you listened to: `plays:>=5` (play count, with ranges and comparisons as for years — songs never played are `plays:0`), `fav:yes` / `fav:no` (favorites), `added:30d` (added in the last 30 days) and `played:1y` (heard in the last year). Write periods with `d` (days), `w` (weeks), `m` (months) or `y` (years).
- You can also search by what the file check found: `check:damaged`, `check:ok`, `check:none` (not checked), `dr:<8` (dynamic range — ranges and comparisons work as for years).
- **Saving a search**: when you type a search in Songs, a **＋** appears at the right of the search field. Click it and give the search a name, and it becomes a smart playlist (see "Playlists").
- Point at the search field to see a short summary of this.

## Up Next
- Open it with the list button at the top right (⌘U). It shows the songs in the order they will really play, with the current song marked.
- Drag entries to change the order, or right-click to move them up or down or to remove them. Double-click an entry to jump to that song.
- **Play Next** (⌘Return) puts songs right after the current one, **Add to Queue** (⌘⇧Return) at the very end. The same song can be added more than once.
- Dropping songs or albums onto the Up Next panel (or, while it is closed, onto the list button at the top right) adds them to the end of the queue.
- **Save** at the top of the queue makes a new playlist of the queue as it is, and **Clear Queue** removes everything.
- Once you have clicked an entry in the queue, the keyboard works there too: ↑↓ to move, Return to play, Delete to remove, ⌥↑ / ⌥↓ to reorder.
- Songs that cannot be played (file missing, damaged) are skipped, and you are told why.

## Playlists
- Create one with **+** in the sidebar or with ⌘N. Right-click songs → **Add to Playlist**.
- Dropping songs or albums onto a playlist in the sidebar adds them too. Dropping them on the "Playlists" title makes a new playlist.
- Double-click a playlist's name (or right-click → **Rename**) to rename it.
- Inside a playlist, drag songs to reorder them. Select songs and press Delete to take them out of the playlist; **Undo** in the notice bar at the bottom brings them back.
- Right-click → **Export as M3U8…**, and File → **Import Playlist…** (or open an `.m3u8` or `.m3u` file with OnEum). Playlists made on older Korean Windows (EUC-KR) are read as well. Network paths that point to another computer are not followed.
- The playlist that is open can also be renamed, exported and deleted from the File menu.
- **Smart playlists** are saved searches (magnifier icon). They do not hold songs: each time one is opened it gathers the songs that match, so it changes by itself as you add music or listen. For example: `genre:jazz format:lossless`, `added:30d` (what came in this month), `plays:0` (songs not heard yet), `fav:yes year:1990-1999`.
  - To make one: search in Songs, then click **＋** in the search field. Up to 100 can be kept.
  - Right-click: **Play**, **Edit Search** (puts the search back into the search field — change it, then save it under the same name with ＋), **Rename**, **Delete**.
  - Inside a smart playlist the search field narrows it further. Songs cannot be dragged into it or reordered.
- Deleting a playlist does not delete any music files.

## Listen Again and Radio
- **Listen Again** (in the list on the left): the four tabs at the top show **Recently Played**, **Recently Added** (newest in the library first), **Most Played** and **Not Heard Lately** (songs you have heard but not in the last 30 days, the longest ago first). Each shows up to 300 songs.
- **Radio** ("Keep Playing When the Queue Ends" in Settings → Playback, off at first): when the last song in the queue starts, five songs from your library that go with it are added to the end of the queue, so the music does not stop.
  Songs close in genre, artist and year come first; songs heard in the last two hours, and more than two songs in a row by one artist, are avoided. Nothing goes online.
  It does nothing while Repeat is on. The songs it adds can be removed or moved in the queue like any others.

## Media Servers on the Network
Music on a media server of the same network (the DLNA/UPnP server of a NAS, say) can be looked through and played without connecting a shared folder.
- **Network** at the left looks for servers. OnEum talks to the network only when this view is opened and when a server is added.
- If no server is found, give its address in **Add by Address…** (a numeric address like `192.168.0.10`, or the address of its device description, starting with `http://`). An added server is remembered; a right-click removes it from the list.
- Click a server for the folders it presents. Click a folder to go in, and the Back button to go up. Double-clicking a song puts the songs of that folder into the queue and plays from that one. A right-click offers **Play Next** and **Add to Queue**, and **Play** and **Add to Queue** above apply to the whole folder.
- A server's songs do not become part of the library. They are not in the song lists, the search or the statistics, and cannot be made favorites or put into playlists. They stay in the queue, so listening can go on the next time OnEum is opened.
- What plays is what the server gives as the original file: FLAC · WAV · AIFF · ALAC · AAC (MP4) · MP3 · Ogg Vorbis · DSD. A song the server only gives converted to another format is shown faint and does not play.
- A song is taken into memory as it arrives, so it plays on if the network drops out for a moment. Volume leveling (ReplayGain) is not applied to a server's songs.

## Song Info (⌘I)
Open it with the ⓘ button at the top right or with ⌘I. It shows the song that is playing; for another song, right-click it → **Song Info** (⌥⌘I).
- Cover art, title, artist, album, track number, year, genre, length, format, sample rate, channels, bit depth, bitrate, file size, volume leveling values, play count, and where the file is (**Show in Finder**).
- **Going further**: click the artist, album or genre to go to that artist's, album's or genre's songs. When several genres are named, each can be clicked on its own.
- **Credits and work details**: when the file names them, the composer, lyricist, conductor, arranger, performers, producer, engineer, mixing and remixer, and the work, movement, label, catalog number, recording date and release date are shown. A name in the credits that is an artist in your library can be clicked.
- **Other Versions of This Song**: if the library has more songs with the same title and artist (on another album, in another format), up to six are listed, the best sounding first. Click one to see its details.
- **Lyrics**: shown when the file's tags contain lyrics, or when an `.lrc` or `.txt` file with the same name as the song is in the same folder. When the lyrics carry times (`.lrc`), the current line is highlighted and followed as the song plays, and clicking a line plays from there. Lyric files saved on older Korean Windows (EUC-KR) are read as well. Lyrics are not looked up on the internet.
- When a lyrics file gives a translation or a pronunciation for the same moment, it is shown in smaller type under the original and highlighted with it.
- **File check results**: for a checked song, **Integrity** (Intact (MD5 matches) / Intact (decodes to the end) / Damaged), **Dynamic range** (the DR value, with the album's) and **True peak** (see "File Check").
- When the volume leveling values end with "(measured by the app)", they were measured by the app rather than read from the file's tags (see "Volume Leveling").

## Signal Path
The **Signal Path** in the Song Info panel shows, in order, what the app does to the song that is playing on its way from the file to the output device. For example: `Source FLAC · 44,100 Hz · 16 bit → Resampling 44,100 → 48,000 Hz → Equalizer Rock → Output MacBook Pro Speakers · 48,000 Hz`.
- The word at the top right sums it up. **Unchanged**: the app does not change the samples. **Adjusted**: features you switched on, such as volume leveling, the equalizer, crossfeed or the app volume, are changing the sound. **Converted**: the sample rate, channels or bit depth are being converted for this device. Point at the word for an explanation.
- The color of the dot tells the kind of step: violet for adjustments you switched on, teal for conversions for the device, amber for peak protection.
- The steps that can appear: DSD to PCM, channel mapping (mono or 5.1/7.1 files to stereo), volume leveling, resampling, equalizer, headphone correction, crossfeed, peak protection, app volume and dither.
- **Peak protection** means the song has moments above full scale (0 dBFS), and the level was lowered slightly for just those moments so that the sound is not clipped and distorted. Songs without such moments are left alone.
- **Dither** means a very small noise was added to prevent the distortion that rounding causes when playing to a 16- or 24-bit device. It is not added when the samples go out as they are.
- "Unchanged" is about what happens inside the app. What comes after it — the operating system, the driver, the DAC — the app cannot see.

## Volume Leveling
Songs are recorded at different loudness; leveling saves you from reaching for the volume all the time. Switch it on in Settings → Playback → **Volume Leveling (ReplayGain)**. It is off at first.
- **Track**: brings every song to the same loudness. Good when you listen to songs from many albums mixed together.
- **Album**: levels the album as a whole, so quiet songs on it stay quiet and loud ones stay loud. Good for listening to whole albums. Songs without an album value use their track value.
- **Pre-amp**: raises or lowers all leveled songs further. Double-click the slider to return it to 0 dB. It does not apply to songs without leveling values.
- **Prevent clipping** (on at first): limits the gain so that leveling does not make the sound distort. Even with it off, moments above full scale are not clipped: "peak protection" brings them down.
- The values come first from the **ReplayGain tags in the file**. Tags another program has written are used as they are.
- **Songs without tags** are played as they are. To level these too, click **Analyze** under Settings → Playback → **Analyze Songs Without Tags**. The app reads each song to its end and measures its loudness (the EBU R 128 broadcast standard, reference −18 LUFS), and uses the result as it would a tag.
  - The number of songs to analyze and the progress are shown. You can keep listening meanwhile. **Stop** stops it, and you can go on later.
  - The results are kept only in the app; **your music files are not changed**.
  - An album without any tags is measured as a whole, so that it also gets "Album" values.
  - A song whose file changes is analyzed again. Songs you add later are not analyzed by themselves; click the button again when you want them leveled.
  - **Values Measured by the App → Clear** removes them all (analyze again to get them back). Songs that could not be read are remembered and passed over by later runs; they are tried again when the file changes, when the app starts again, or after Clear.
- Tags written by older programs (mp3gain and the like) were measured in another way, so next to songs the app measured they may sound 1 to 2 dB different. Songs with tags always follow their tags.
- Whether leveling is applied is shown as a "Volume leveling" step in the song's **Signal Path**. The VU meter shows the leveled level too.

## Equalizer (⌘E)
Open it with the slider button at the top, **View → Equalizer…**, or **Equalizer → Open** in Settings → Playback. The button lights up in the accent color while the equalizer is changing the sound.
- **Ten bands** (32 Hz to 16 kHz, an octave apart) and a **pre-amp**, each within ±12 dB in steps of 0.5 dB. Drag or click to set them; **double-click to return to 0 dB**. When a slider has the focus, ↑↓ move it a step at a time.
- The curve at the top shows how much the current values raise or lower the sound at each frequency. Each band sits right above its slider.
- **Presets**: Flat, Bass Boost, Bass Reducer, Treble Boost, Vocal, Loudness (for low volume), Pop, Rock, Jazz, Classical, Electronic and Acoustic. Choosing one switches the equalizer on. Moving a slider makes it "Custom".
- **My Presets**: type a name and click **Save as My Preset** to keep the current values under that name; it appears at the bottom of the preset list (up to 40). Saving again under the same name replaces the values. With one of your presets chosen, **Delete This Preset** is shown.
- The switch at the top right turns it on and off. **Reset** returns every value to 0 dB. When it is off, or every value is 0 dB, the sound is not touched at all.
- Changes are heard within a second (once the audio decoded ahead has played). The sound glides to new values instead of jumping.
- Raising bands can push the sound above full scale. Lowering the pre-amp by as much as you raised keeps it below (the built-in presets are set that way, so they sound a little quieter); moments that still go above are not clipped but brought down by "peak protection".

## Headphone Correction
Every headphone model has its own frequency response, so the same music sounds bass-heavy on one and sharp on another. This feature corrects that with filters made from measurements. Use it in Settings → Playback → **Headphone Correction** (off at first).
- Choose your headphone under **Correction Profile**, and the correction is applied at once. **Built-in profiles** for 35 widely used models are included.
  - Headphones: Sennheiser HD 800 S · HD 600 · HD 650 · HD 660S2 · HD 560S · Momentum 4 Wireless · Momentum 5 Wireless, Beyerdynamic DT 770 Pro · DT 990 Pro · DT 1990 (balanced earpads), AKG K701 · K371 · K712, Audio-Technica ATH-M50x · ATH-R70x, Sony MDR-7506 · WH-1000XM4 · WH-1000XM5, Apple AirPods Max, Bose QuietComfort 45, HIFIMAN Sundara (post-2020 earpads) · HE400se · Edition XS, Focal Clear · Utopia, Audeze LCD-X (2021) · LCD-2 Classic, Meze 99 Classics, Koss Porta Pro.
  - In-ear: Sony WF-1000XM4, Samsung Galaxy Buds2 Pro, Etymotic ER2XR, Moondrop Aria, Sennheiser IE 600.
- The values of the built-in profiles are the ones the AutoEq project computed from oratory1990's measurements and published (to the Harman target), included unchanged. They come from the one unit that was measured, so units of the same model can differ a little, as can the result with worn earpads or a different fit. Names that mention earpads or a year were measured in that configuration. The Sennheiser Momentum 5 Wireless alone has no values published by AutoEq yet: its values were computed with the AutoEq program, to the same target, from the measurement Super* Review has published (with noise cancelling on), on 2026-11-16. It was measured by someone else on a different rig than the other profiles, and before the product came out.
- For a headphone that is not listed, get a **correction file**. Widely used are the `ParametricEQ.txt` files the AutoEq project has made for each model (text files in the Equalizer APO format). The app does not fetch them from the internet.
- Choose a file with **Import…**: a profile named after the file is made and applied at once (`Sennheiser HD 600 ParametricEQ.txt` → "Sennheiser HD 600"). Importing the same name again replaces its values. Up to 30 can be kept. Imported profiles are listed under "Imported" at the top of the list; one with the same name as a built-in profile is used in its place.
- Under the chosen profile, its number of filters and its pre-amp are shown. When you do not want a correction, choose **Off**. **Delete** removes imported profiles only (built-in profiles cannot be deleted).
- The supported filters are **PK** (peaking), **LSC** (low shelf) and **HSC** (high shelf); AutoEq's files use only these three. A file with any other kind of filter is not imported at all, rather than in part (that would be a different correction).
- The `Preamp` value of a file lowers everything by as much as the filters raise, so that the sound does not go above full scale. That is why switching it on makes the sound a little quieter. If the filters raise more than the pre-amp makes room for, you are told so, and moments above full scale are brought down by "peak protection".
- A file whose filters together raise any frequency by more than 12 dB is applied with its pre-amp lowered by the difference (so that a badly made file cannot suddenly be very loud). Real correction files stay well within this limit.
- It is applied after the equalizer and before crossfeed, and works together with the equalizer (the correction is for your headphones, the equalizer for your taste). Changing it glides over without a jump.
- While it is on, the sound differs from the original. The Signal Path shows "Headphone correction" with the profile's name. Leave it off for speakers.

## Headphone Crossfeed
Switch it on in Settings → Playback → **Headphones** (off at first).
- Mixes a little of each channel's low end into the other, so that headphones sound more like sitting in front of speakers. It eases listening to older recordings with instruments hard on one side.
- Choose **Light · Medium · Strong**. Sounds in the centre (such as vocals) hardly change.
- Leave it off for speakers.

## VU Meter
Open it with the meter button at the top right or with ⌘⇧U (View → Show/Hide VU Meter). Analog meters for the left and right channels appear side by side above the player bar, their needles showing the level of the music playing.
- The **spectrum display** between the two meters shows with bars what is sounding right now, from the low end (left) to the high end (right). The numbers below are frequencies in Hz.
- Next to the meters, the **Source** (file format) and **Output** (device, sample rate, processing) of the song playing are shown. The meters can stay open together with Up Next or Song Info.
- Drag the top edge of the meter strip to change its size.
- The scale runs from −20 to +3 VU; above 0 is the red zone. Most current records move around 0, quiet recordings around −10.
- 0 VU is −11 dBFS RMS (a sine wave peaking at −8 dBFS). The PEAK lamp lights when samples reach full scale.
- The needles follow the level of the music itself, so they move even when the app volume is down or muted. With volume leveling on, they show the leveled level.
- When playback pauses, the needles slowly return to the left.

## Output Quality
Options for listening through a DAC or good headphones (Settings → Playback). All three are off at first, and while they are off OnEum does not change any device setting.

| Setting | What it does | Good to know |
|---|---|---|
| **Match the Song's Sample Rate** | Sets the device to each song's sample rate (44.1 kHz, 96 kHz …) and plays it without converting (resampling). If the device does not offer that rate, it uses a whole multiple of it, else the next higher rate, else the device's highest rate, and converts to that. | Moving to a song with another sample rate pauses for under a second while the device changes. Songs of the same rate (the same album) follow without a gap. The device setting applies to other apps too. |
| **Exclusive Access** | While it plays, only OnEum uses the device. Alert sounds and other apps are not mixed in, and other apps cannot change the device's settings. | While playing, other apps' sound does not come out of that device (video calls, the browser …). |
| **Memory Playback** | Loads the file being played and the next one into memory ahead of time. Playback goes on even if an external drive goes to sleep or a network folder slows down. | Uses as much memory as the files being played (at most two, the next one included). Files larger than 1 GB are read from the disk as usual. |

- If a device setting was changed, it is **put back and exclusive access is released** when the queue ends, playback stops, or playback stays paused for more than 30 seconds. The same happens when you quit.
- How a song is being played right now can be seen in the **Signal Path** of the Song Info panel (⌘I) and in **Output** and **Processing** on the meter deck. For example: `Output DAC · 96,000 Hz · 24 bit · exclusive`, `Processing: None`.
  When the Signal Path says "Unchanged" (and the meter deck's processing says "None"), the app sends the samples from the file out as they are. Turning the app volume below 100% or switching on volume leveling or the equalizer is shown there.
- When the sample rate has to be converted, the conversion is flat up to 20 kHz. When converting a master made as loud as it can be, or playing MP3 or AAC, produces moments above full scale, "peak protection" brings down just those moments.
- Leaving the app volume at 100% and setting the level on the DAC or amplifier keeps the samples untouched.

## File Check
In Settings → Library, **Check** under **File Check** reads every music file to its end. Files are only read; the results stay in the app's database, and a file that changes is checked again on its own.
- **Integrity**: whether the file decodes to its end; for FLAC, whether the audio matches the MD5 the file carries (a checksum of the original audio); for formats that state their length exactly (FLAC, ALAC, WAV, AIFF, DSD), whether the end is missing. An MP3's stated length cannot be trusted, so an MP3 cut short is not told apart.
- **Dynamic range (DR)**: the difference between the loud and the average sound of the music. Higher means a less compressed recording. Worked out the way dr.loudness-war.info describes; other meters can differ by about 1.
- **True peak**: the highest level of the waveform, between the samples too (dBTP). Above 0 it may clip when converted to analogue.
- The results show in Song Info and can be searched (`check:damaged`, `dr:<8`); **Show Damaged** lists the damaged files. **Clear** removes all results.

## Moving Over from Apple Music
In Apple Music, save an XML file with **File ▸ Library ▸ Export Library…**, then choose it in Settings → Library under **Apple Music Library → Import…**.
- The songs that are in your library (found by their file) get their play counts, last plays and dates added; loved songs become favorites. Play counts are not added up — the higher one is kept — so importing again changes nothing.
- The playlists you made are created (not smart playlists or folders). When a list of that name exists already, "(Apple Music)" is added to the name; a list imported before is not made again.
- Songs that are not in your library are counted, not added. Music files are not changed.

## Taking Your History to Another Device
Under **Library Data** in Settings → Library, **Export…** saves one file; in OnEum on another device, choose that file with **Import…**. The file can also simply be kept.

- The file holds play counts, the plays themselves (what the statistics and Listen Again go by), dates added, favorites, playlists and smart playlists. Settings such as appearance and output, and what is pinned, are not part of it.
- Songs are found by their folders and file names below the music folder, which fits a device that holds a copy of the music folder. Songs that are not in the library, and songs that cannot be told apart because the same name occurs more than once, are skipped and counted.
- An import merges: the higher play count, the later last play and the earlier date added are kept, and importing the same file twice changes nothing more. When a playlist of that name exists already, "(2)" is added to the name. Music files are not changed.

## Settings (⌘,)
The gear button at the top right opens them too. Close them with Esc, the close (×) button, or a click outside. Changes apply and are saved at once.
- **General**: language (Follow System / 한국어 / English), appearance (Follow System / Light / Dark), and the Format column.
- **Library**: each music folder with its number of songs and when it was last scanned. The magnifier button opens the folder in Finder, and the trash button removes the folder from the library (you are asked first, and the original files stay where they are). **Add Music Folder**, **Rescan**. Below them, the file check and moving over from Apple Music.
- **Playback**, from the top: output device (with the device and sample rate in use), output quality, volume leveling and the analysis of songs without tags, playing order (shuffle by album, resume long tracks), equalizer, headphone crossfeed, headphone correction and radio.
- **Shortcuts**: the keyboard shortcuts.
- **About**: version, opening this guide, showing the log and data folders, and the open source licenses. Click **Show** next to the licenses to unfold the list of components as a table below it; click a source address to open it in the browser.

## Supported Files
MP3, MP2 (.mp2), FLAC, WAV (ADPCM included), AIFF, ALAC (.m4a), AAC (.m4a), Ogg Vorbis (.ogg), DSD (.dsf, .dff) and Matroska/WebM audio (.mka, .webm).
- **Between songs**: FLAC, WAV, AIFF, ALAC, MP3 and AAC follow each other on an album without gaps. For AAC, the silence the encoder adds at the start and end is removed using what the file says about it (an edit list, or Apple's gapless information). Ogg Vorbis plays for the length recorded in the file; files from some encoders may leave a very short gap between songs.
- **DSD**: DSF and DSDIFF (.dff), DSD64 to DSD512. As most players do, OnEum converts DSD to PCM (88.2 kHz; 96 kHz for the 48 kHz family), so volume leveling, the equalizer, the meters and sample-rate matching all work as usual. The conversion filter is flat to 20 kHz and removes the noise DSD pushes above the audio band. No gain is added, so the SACD reference level (0 dB) comes out at −6 dBFS and DSD may sound quieter than CDs ("Analyze Songs Without Tags" in volume leveling evens that out). DSD songs on an album follow each other without gaps: the end of one and the start of the next are converted as one. Sending DSD to the DAC as it is (DoP or native DSD) and DST-compressed .dff files are not supported. Song Info shows the file's DSD sample rate, and the signal path a "DSD to PCM" step.
- **Matroska and WebM**: the FLAC, Vorbis, AAC, MP3 or PCM inside is played and the file's tags are read. WebM with Opus inside is not played.
- **CUE sheets**: when a whole album is one file (FLAC, WAV …) and a `.cue` file next to it points to that file, the album is added as the songs the sheet lists (the file itself does not appear on its own).
  Titles, artists, album, genre, year and ReplayGain come from the sheet; what the sheet leaves out comes from the file's tags. A song starts at the sheet's `INDEX 01` and ends at the next song's `INDEX 01`, and the songs follow each other without gaps.
  Korean in a sheet is read in UTF-8 and in EUC-KR. The file is found also when the sheet spells its name in other letter case, or with Korean letters composed differently. If the sheet names a `.wav` and only a `.flac` of the same name is there (converted later), that is used. Opening that file from the Finder opens the sheet's songs. If the sheet describes a longer recording than the file holds, only the songs within the file are added; if none of them is (another disc's sheet, say), the file is added as one song as it is. If the file was a favourite or in playlists before the sheet came, the sheet's songs take its place there. Editing or deleting a sheet takes effect right away (folder watching); after a delete the file is one song again.
  When every song has its own file and the sheet points to those files (as CD ripping programs often write it), the sheet is not used and the files are added as they are. If two sheets point to one file, the first by name is used.
  In File Check, a FLAC's MD5 covers the whole file, so it is not compared for the songs of a sheet (whether they decode to the end is still checked). In an M3U8 export, the songs of a sheet are written as paths only OnEum reads (`Album.cue/02`), which other players cannot open. OnEum reads such a playlist back in (also where the music folder has not been added yet: it finds the sheet and registers the songs).
- **High resolution**: 24-bit, 96 and 192 kHz files play. If the device takes that sample rate they go out as they are, otherwise they are converted to suit the device (see "Output Quality").
- **Mono and surround**: mono goes to both sides; 5.1 and 7.1 files (FLAC, WAV, AIFF, ALAC) are mixed down to stereo. The centre and surround channels are added a little lower to front left and right, the low-frequency effects (LFE) channel is left out, and the result is kept from going above full scale. Surround is not played out as surround.
- **What cannot be played**: copy-protected (DRM) files (.m4p and the like), Opus, WavPack, DST-compressed DSD, and among AAC files HE-AAC (AAC+) and 5.1-channel AAC. Such files are not added to the library; the notice at the end of a scan says how many unsupported files were skipped.
- **Tags and cover art**: title, artist, album, track number, year, genre, ReplayGain, lyrics, credits and embedded cover art are read. Without embedded art, a `cover` or `folder` image in the same folder is used. Files without tags are shown by their file name. Tags written on older Korean Windows (EUC-KR) are read as Korean.

## Troubleshooting
| Situation | What it means and what to do |
|---|---|
| A folder was added but stays at "Scanning" | macOS asks for permission for the Downloads, Documents and Desktop folders and for external drives. Click **Allow** in the window it shows. If you declined, allow OnEum in System Settings → Privacy & Security → Files and Folders, then rescan in Settings → Library. |
| Files in a folder do not appear in the list | They are in a format that is not supported (Opus, WavPack, HE-AAC, 5.1 AAC, DST-compressed DSD, DRM files … — see "Supported Files"). The notice at the end of the scan gives the number left out. |
| A song is dimmed and marked ⚠ | Its file cannot be found. Reconnect the external drive or put the file back where it was, and the song comes back by itself. Files that were only renamed or moved within the same folder are linked again at the next scan. |
| A "Skipped" notice | That song could not be played, so playback moved on to the next. The notice gives the reason (file not found, damaged, unsupported format). |
| "The output device was disconnected. Playback is paused." | Your headphones or DAC were unplugged. Playback stopped so that the sound does not suddenly come out of the speakers. Click **Play on Default Device**, or reconnect the device and press play. |
| "The output device is not responding, so playback cannot be controlled." | The output device (or its driver) has stopped responding. Reconnect it or choose another device, then restart OnEum. Quitting may take a few seconds. |
| The chosen output device is missing | Choose "System Default" or another device in Settings → Playback. |
| Other apps are silent while OnEum plays | Settings → Playback → **Exclusive Access** is on. Switch it off, or stop OnEum (or leave it paused for more than 30 seconds), and other apps can use the device again. |
| A short break when the song changes | **Match the Song's Sample Rate** is on and the two songs have different sample rates, so the device is being changed. With it off there is no break, and songs are converted to the device's sample rate instead. |
| Leveling is on, but some songs are still louder or quieter | Those songs have no leveling values (ReplayGain "None" in Song Info). Run Settings → Playback → **Analyze Songs Without Tags**. If they have values and still sound different, the tags may be of the older kind (see "Volume Leveling"). |
| Only one song played, then playback stopped | **Stop After Current Song** was set ("Stops after song" next to the volume). Press play to go on with the next song. |
| The sound faded out and stopped while listening | The **Sleep Timer** ran out. Press play to go on from where it stopped, at your usual volume. |
| A smart playlist is empty | No songs match it at the moment. Right-click → **Edit Search** to check the search. A value that cannot be understood (`year:abc`, `fav:maybe`) finds nothing. |
| A correction file cannot be imported | The notice says why. "a filter that is not supported" means it contains filters other than PK, LSC and HSC; "this line could not be understood" means that line has another form (it should look like `Filter 1: ON PK Fc 105 Hz Gain -3.5 dB Q 0.70`). Choose `ParametricEQ.txt`, not the file for graphic equalizers (`GraphicEQ.txt`). |
| The sound is odd (too much bass, a narrow stereo image) | Check whether the equalizer, headphone correction or headphone crossfeed is on. The **Signal Path** in Song Info lists everything that is being applied. |
| No lyrics are shown | The file's tags hold no lyrics, and there is no `.lrc` or `.txt` with the song's name in its folder. Name the lyrics file exactly like the song file, with only the extension different. |
| An album appears split in two | When an album's files are spread over several folders and only some of them have an "album artist" tag, the album can be split. Make the tags match or put the files in one folder, and it comes together. |
| Titles or artists look garbled, like `°¡»ç` | The tags were written on older Korean Windows. OnEum reads such text as Korean; if songs scanned by an earlier version still look garbled, **Rescan** (⌘R) puts them right. Songs that still look garbled may be in an old encoding of a language other than Korean. |
| A notice that the library data was damaged | A new library was made. Add your music folders again. The damaged file is kept in `~/Library/Application Support/OnEum/`. |

## Where Your Data Is
- Library and settings: `~/Library/Application Support/OnEum/` — the song list, favorites, play counts, playlists and smart playlists, the queue, the loudness the app measured, equalizer presets, headphone correction profiles and the columns you chose. Deleting this folder starts OnEum afresh; your music files are not affected. Other user accounts on the same Mac cannot read this folder.
- Album art cache: `~/Library/Caches/OnEum/` (can be deleted; it is made again).
- Log: `~/Library/Logs/OnEum/oneum.log`
- Settings → About opens the data and log folders directly.
