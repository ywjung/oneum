# OnEum User Guide (Android)

## Adding music
OnEum reads the music files on your phone where they are. Once, at the start, it has to be given the right to read files.

- **The right**: press **Allow Access** on the first page, and on the settings page that opens allow OnEum **All files access**. It is used for reading music files only.
- **The Music folder**: music in the phone's **Music** folder appears by itself. Put music there by connecting the phone to a computer with a cable, or with a file manager.
- **Other folders**: **Settings → Library → Add Folder** adds another folder, also one on a card or on a drive that is plugged in. The top of the storage and the Download folder itself cannot be chosen; folders inside them can.
- **Single files**: **Settings → Library → Open Music Files** plays the files you choose without adding their folder. They are not copied but read where they are, and the songs stay in the library. Of a file that cannot be read where it is (one on a cloud drive, say) OnEum keeps a copy, and deletes the copy when the song is removed from the library (the original stays as it is).

- Music that was added appears by itself within half a minute or so while OnEum is on the screen. To read it at once, press **Settings → Library → Rescan**.
- **Remove** leaves a folder's files as they are. Its songs only leave OnEum's lists.
- A music file in another app can also be opened with **Open with → OnEum**.
- OnEum only reads music files. It never changes or deletes files or their tags. Favourites, play counts and playlists are kept inside OnEum only.
- No account and no internet connection are needed (lyrics alone are also looked for on the internet if you switch that on in Settings).

## The screen
- **Tabs at the bottom**: Library · Search · Playlists · Settings. Pressing the open tab again returns to its first page.
- **Library**: Songs · Albums · Artists · Genres · Favorites · Listen Again · Folders · Statistics. What you have pinned comes first, under "Pinned".
- **Pinning**: press and hold an album, artist, genre or playlist (or press ⋯ at the top right of its page) and choose **Pin to Library**: it is kept at the top of the library (up to 12). **Unpin** is in the same place.
- **‹** at the top left goes back to the page before. So does the phone's **Back** (a swipe from the edge of the screen, or the Back button). On the first page, Back puts OnEum behind what was there before, and the music plays on.
- In a long list, hold the right edge of the screen and drag up or down to move through it quickly.
- **The bar above the tabs**: the song that is playing. Press it to open the player on the whole screen.
- **Searching inside a list**: the magnifier at the top right of a list (songs, albums, artists, a playlist and so on) searches in that list only. Press the magnifier again to end the search.
- **Landscape**: turn the phone on its side and the screen turns with it (while auto-rotate is on). The player has the cover at the left and the buttons at the right, and the bar of the song that is playing moves beside the tabs.
- **Widget**: the "Now Playing" widget on the home screen shows cover, title and artist with previous · play/pause · next. Press and hold an empty place on the home screen, choose **Widgets** and pick OnEum. While OnEum is running the buttons act at once and the app does not come to the front. After OnEum was swiped away in the list of recent apps the last song stays, and a press opens OnEum. A press on the cover or the title opens OnEum too.

## Listening
- Pressing a song in a list puts the whole list into the queue and plays from that song. **Play** and **Shuffle** above a list start from the top or in a shuffled order.
- **⋯** at the right of a song (or a long press on it): Play Next · Add to Queue · Add to Playlist · Favorite · Go to Album · Go to Artist · Song Info · Show in Files · Remove. A removed song leaves the library and the playlists only; its file stays. A scan (as at every start) does not bring it back; **Settings → Library → Bring Back Removed Songs** takes all of them in again.
- A long press on an album, artist, genre or playlist: Play · Play Next · Add to Queue · Add to Playlist · Pin.
- **The player**: cover, title (press it for the album), heart (favourite), position, shuffle · previous · play · next · repeat, app volume. Under shuffle and repeat is what each stands at, in words (Shuffle Off · Shuffle On; Repeat Off · Repeat All · Repeat One); one that is on is coloured and stands on a tinted ground. **⌄** at the top left closes it, and so does pulling the cover down.
- The three buttons at the top right of the player choose what is shown in the cover's place: **Lyrics · VU Meter · Up Next**. Pressing the same button again brings the cover back.
- **Lyrics**: the lyrics in the file's tags or in an .lrc file of the same name. With time stamps the line being sung is followed, and pressing a line plays from there.
- **Find Lyrics Online** (Settings → General → Lyrics, off at first): while it is on, a song whose lyrics view is opened and whose file has no lyrics with time stamps is looked for on LRCLIB (lrclib.net); what is found is shown with the note "Lyrics found online (LRCLIB)" under it. Only the song's title, artist, album and length are sent (the service can see the internet address the request comes from), and what comes back is kept on the device, so the same song is not asked for twice. While it is off, nothing is asked.
- **VU Meter**: needles for the loudness of the left and right channel, a spectrum, and the file's format with the path the sound takes.
- **Up Next**: press a song to play from it; ⋯ removes it from the queue. Drag the **≡** handle at the right of a song to move it. **Save** keeps the queue as a playlist, **Clear Queue** empties it. Press **Select** and choose several songs: **Selected Songs…** moves them to the top, adds them to a playlist or takes them out of the queue, all at once.
- A menu that ⋯ brought up closes when you press outside it or pull its handle down.
- **Sleep Timer** and **Stop After Current Song** are under ⋯ of the song that is playing. While one is on it is shown at the bottom of the player; press those words to switch it off.
- **Repeating a section (A–B)** is under ⋯ of the song that is playing too. The section from where you choose **Repeat Section: Start Here (A)** to where you choose **Repeat Section: End Here (B)** is played over and over; **Stop Repeating Section** ends it. While it is on, the section is shown at the bottom of the player (pressing those words ends it too) and both places are marked on the seek bar. Another song ends it.
- **Show in Songs**, under ⋯ of the song that is playing, opens the song list at the place of that song.
- **Song Info** (under ⋯ of a song): album, artist and genres (press one to go there), track number, length, format, leveling values, what the file check found, play count, what the file names besides (the label, and credits such as composer, lyricist and conductor), other versions of the same song in the library, for the song that is playing the signal path, and the folder the file is in. **Show in Files** opens that folder in the phone's Files app.
- Press the time at the right of the position to switch between the whole length and the time left. Press the speaker at the left of the volume to mute; press it again for the volume as it was.
- Playback goes on with the screen off and while another app is used. The notification, the lock screen and the buttons of headphones play, pause and skip. For about ten minutes after a pause the notification and the lock screen can still play on; after that, open OnEum to go on listening. The first time something plays you are asked whether notifications are allowed; music plays without them too. Swiping OnEum away in the list of recent apps ends playback.
- A phone call pauses the music, and it goes on when the call is over. When headphones are pulled out the music pauses rather than coming out of the speaker.
- Loudness is set with the phone's volume buttons. **App volume** in the player turns down OnEum's own sound only.

## Song lists
- **Sort**: above the lists of Songs, Favorites, a genre, a folder and a smart playlist, **Sort** puts the songs in order of title, artist, album, year, length, play count, rating, date added, last played or format. Choosing the same one again turns the order round; **Default Order** brings the usual one back.
- **Ratings**: under ⋯ of a song, press its rating (**No Rating** at first) to give it 1 to 5 stars; the stars in Song Info can be pressed too. Ratings are kept inside OnEum only, and files are not changed. **Sort** above a list can order by rating, and a search can ask for it: `rating:>=4`.
- **Select**: press **Select** above a list and press songs to choose several. **Selected Songs…** plays them, plays them next, adds them to the queue or to a playlist, makes them favourites or removes them, all at once. **Select All** chooses every song; **Done** ends it.
- A play is counted once half of a song (four minutes of a long one) has really been heard. A song that is skipped is not counted.

## Search
- The **Search** tab finds by title, artist and album.
- Below the field, choose what to look for: **Songs · Albums · Artists**. Moving the results up or down puts the keyboard away.
- A search can be narrowed to a field: `artist:beatles` · `album:…` · `genre:jazz` · `year:1990-1999` · `format:flac` · `fav:yes` · `plays:>=5` · `added:30d` · `check:damaged` · `dr:<8`.
- A search can also say the order and the number of what it finds: `sort:plays` (most played first) · `sort:added` · `sort:rating` · `sort:-title` (`-` turns the order round) · `limit:50`. Kept as a smart playlist it makes a list such as "the 25 most played of four stars and more" (`rating:>=4 sort:plays limit:25`).
- **Keeping a search**: search for songs, press **＋** in the search field and give it a name: the search becomes a smart playlist.

## Playlists
- **＋** at the top right of the **Playlists** tab makes a new playlist. So does ⋯ → **Add to Playlist** on a song.
- Open a playlist and press **⋯** at the top right (or press and hold it in the list): Play · Play Next · Add to Queue · Pin · Rename · Export as M3U8 · Delete.
- Inside a playlist, drag the **≡** handle at the right of a song to move it. A song taken out with ⋯ → **Remove from Playlist** comes back with **Undo** in the message below.
- A **smart playlist** is a saved search. It gathers the songs that match each time it is opened. ⋯ → **Edit Search** brings its search into the Search tab; change it and press ＋ to save it under the same name. Rename and Delete are under ⋯ too.
- **Settings → Library → Import Playlist** reads an M3U8 file. **Export as M3U8** asks where to keep the file.

## Media servers on the network
- **Library → Network** looks for media servers on the same network (the DLNA server of a NAS, say). OnEum talks to the network only when this page is opened and when a server is added. While the library has no music, the **Media Servers on the Network** button of the first page opens it.
- If the server does not appear, give its address in **Add by Address…** (a numeric address like `192.168.0.10`). An added server is remembered; press and hold it to remove it from the list.
- Press a server for the folders it presents. Pressing a song puts the songs of that folder into the queue and plays from that one; press and hold for **Play Next** and **Add to Queue**. **Play** and **Add to Queue** above apply to the whole folder.
- A server's songs do not become part of the library. They are not in the song lists, the search or the statistics, and cannot be made favorites or put into playlists. They stay in the queue, so listening can go on the next time OnEum is opened.
- What plays is what the server gives as the original file: FLAC · WAV · AIFF · ALAC · AAC (MP4) · MP3 · Ogg Vorbis · Opus · WavPack · Monkey's Audio (APE) · DSD. A song is taken into memory as it arrives, and volume leveling (ReplayGain) is not applied to a server's songs.
- **Network folders (WebDAV)**: a folder that a NAS shares over WebDAV can be looked through and played. In **Add a Network Folder…** enter the folder's address (like `https://nas.local:5006/music/`), a user name and a password, and press **Connect**. An added folder is remembered under "Network Folders"; a long press removes it from the list (and its password with it). Inside, folders and music files are shown as for a media server (a song's title is its file name), and the songs do not become part of the library.
- **The password, and how safe the connection is**: the password is kept only in the phone, encrypted (the key is in the Android Keystore, where the app itself cannot read it). It is in neither the settings file, the log nor a file of the library's data. An `http://` address is not encrypted, so use an `https://` address if you can. If the NAS uses a certificate it made itself, the first connection shows the certificate's fingerprint: compare it with the one your NAS shows in its own settings and press **Trust and Connect**; from then on the app connects only while the server shows that certificate. SMB shares cannot be opened yet.

## Settings
- **Library**: Rescan, Open Music Files, importing a playlist, the music folders (adding, removing, and **Show** in the Files app), and the **file check**: it reads every music file to the end and finds damage, dynamic range and true peak. **Show Damaged** lists the damaged files, if there are any.
- **Importing an Apple Music library** (Settings → Library): save an XML file on the Mac with **File → Library → Export Library** in Apple Music, move it to the phone and choose it: play counts, last played, date added, loved songs and playlists are brought over. Songs are found by the folder they are in and their file name, so this fits songs whose folders were copied from the Mac as they are. Music files are not changed.
- **Exporting and importing your history** (**Library Data** under Settings → Library): **Export to a File** saves play counts, the plays themselves, dates added, favorites, playlists and smart playlists as one file, and **Import from a File** merges a file exported by OnEum on another device. Songs are found by their folder and file name, and play counts are not added up: the higher one is kept. Settings and what is pinned are not part of it, and music files are not changed.
- **Playback**: volume leveling (ReplayGain) and the analysis of songs without tags, shuffle by album, resuming long tracks, a shuffle that favors higher ratings, crossfade (songs that follow each other by themselves overlap for 2 to 10 seconds; songs of one album go on without a gap as before), playback speed (0.5× to 2× at the song's own pitch; while it is not 1× it is shown at the bottom of the player, and pressing those words sets 1× again), crossfeed, headphone correction (35 built-in profiles, correction files, and profiles made on screen band by band), radio, memory playback.
- **Room Correction** (Settings → Playback → Room Correction, for speakers): a correction filter made by measuring your room and speakers (an impulse response file — WAV or FLAC, up to 6 seconds) is put in with **Import…** and applied to everything. The app keeps its own copy, and turns the filter down as a whole so that no frequency gets louder than it was. It can be switched off and on; leave it off for earphones.
- **Equalizer**: ten bands and presets. Double-tap a slider to return it to 0 dB.
- **General**: language and appearance (light · dark · system).
- **Shortcuts**: the list of the shortcuts of a keyboard (see "Keyboard").
- **About**: the version, the open source licenses, and **Save the Log**: it saves a copy of the records to look at when something goes wrong, where you want it. If the app ever ended with an error, **Errors of Earlier Runs** is shown here. **Copy the Record** copies the version, that record, the warnings and errors of the log and the memory all at once: paste it when you report a problem. When the phone takes away the device the screen is drawn with, so that nothing more can be drawn, the app opens again by itself and goes on playing the song; while the way of drawing is Automatic, it draws in the other way from then on. Under **Drawing the Screen** you can choose the way yourself (Automatic · Vulkan · OpenGL ES · glow; changing it starts the app anew). glow is a trial that uses OpenGL ES through another drawing library. **Memory** shows in numbers what the app holds of memory at the moment.

## Keyboard
A keyboard that is plugged into the phone or paired with it has these shortcuts.

- **Playback**: Space plays and pauses · Ctrl+→ / Ctrl+← next / previous song · Ctrl+Alt+→ / Ctrl+Alt+← five seconds on / back · Ctrl+Shift+. stops after the current song · Ctrl+B repeats a section (start → end → off) · Ctrl+↑ / Ctrl+↓ the app's volume · Ctrl+Shift+M mute · Ctrl+S shuffle · Ctrl+Shift+R repeat.
- **The song that is playing**: Ctrl+D favorite · Ctrl+I Song Info · Ctrl+L Show in Songs · Ctrl+Shift+F Show in Files.
- **Screens**: Ctrl+F search · Ctrl+U the queue · Ctrl+Shift+U the VU meters · Ctrl+E the equalizer · Ctrl+, Settings · Esc back.
- **Library**: Ctrl+O Open Music Files · Ctrl+Shift+O Add Folder · Ctrl+N new playlist · Ctrl+R Rescan.
- While text is being typed, Space and the arrow keys belong to the text. The keys do not choose songs in a list; the shortcuts about a song apply to the one that is playing.

## What differs from the Mac and Windows versions
- Android decides where the sound goes and at which sample rate. There is no choice of output device, no output at the song's sample rate and no exclusive output.
- There is no menu bar, and nothing can be dragged onto a window. The columns of a list cannot be chosen either (what they show is in Song Info). Show in Files does not point at a single file: it opens the folder the file is in.
- When an Apple Music library is imported, a Mac finds the songs by where their files are; on a phone they are elsewhere, so they are found by their folder and file name. Where the same folder and file name occur more than once, that song is skipped.
- Screen readers (TalkBack) are not supported yet, and there are no screens made for tablets.
